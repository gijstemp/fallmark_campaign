Four hundred years ago, in a single night, the smaller moon, the Broken Daughter, cracked from within. In four minutes, it fell apart. Most of what broke loose burned up in the sky. What didn't, struck Fallmark.

Five great fragments survived intact enough to reshape entire regions when they landed: the Verdant Interior's forest, the Ironpeak's jagged peaks, the Central Plains' fertile heart, the Deepwater Archipelago's scattered islands, and Crown Mountain itself, standing alone at the continent's center. Dozens of smaller fragments scattered everywhere else, some beautiful, some ruined, some still fought over.

## The Fragment Wars

Nobody went to war over the Fracturing itself. They went to war over what it left behind: suddenly precious land, suddenly valuable resources, borders that used to make sense and stopped making sense overnight. Sixty brutal years followed, ending not in victory for anyone but in exhaustion. The peace that came after has held for two hundred years, built on agreements that everyone who actually signed them is long dead.

It is, by most accounts, a thin peace. It has held. That isn't the same as being solid.

## Why It Happened: Four Theories

Nobody agrees on what actually caused the Fracturing. Four explanations circulate, and which one a person believes usually says as much about their politics as their astronomy.

<div class="card-grid">
<div class="card">
<h3>The Exhaustion Theory</h3>
<p>The moon was simply unstable, geologically, and it broke on its own. The most popular explanation, and the most comforting: nothing to fear, nothing to fix, nothing anyone could have prevented.</p>
</div>
<div class="card">
<h3>The Collision Theory</h3>
<p>Something struck it from outside. Has real physical evidence behind it, and a deeply unsettling implication: if something hit the Broken Daughter once, it could hit the Warden too.</p>
</div>
<div class="card">
<h3>The Sacrifice Theory</h3>
<p>The moon gave itself, deliberately, and the fragments are a gift. This is the position of the Fragment Faithful, a genuine and widespread faith. Restoration, in this view, would be a rejection of that gift.</p>
</div>
<div class="card">
<h3>The Sealed Truth</h3>
<p>Something was sealed inside the moon, and the Fracturing let it out. Held by a small, chronically underfunded, and widely mocked scholarly faction called the Reclaimers. Nobody takes them very seriously.</p>
</div>
</div>

Which of these your character believes (or whether they've ever thought about it at all) is entirely up to you.

## Living With It

Most people don't spend their days thinking about any of this. The Broken Daughter is just the sky. The fragment sites are just geography: some are farmland, some are dangerous ruins worth a contract, some are simply where you're from. Four hundred years is a long time to live with a mystery before it stops feeling urgent and starts just feeling like the world.

That said, adventuring bands spend more time around fragment sites than almost anyone else in Fallmark. If there's more to the story, your characters are exactly the kind of people who'd eventually find out.
