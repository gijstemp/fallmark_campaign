The gods of Aethermere are real. They are not people.

They're better understood as ten fundamental forces — patterns old enough and vast enough that they form the background frequency of reality itself. They don't watch. They don't judge. They don't have favorites. They simply *are*, and everything that exists resonates against them in one way or another.

What they do have is **responsiveness** — not to prayer as a request, but to genuine alignment. A life lived in deep harmony with one of these forces creates a kind of resonance the force responds to, and that response is what every culture in the world calls divine favor, answered prayer, or a cleric's power.

## Every Faith Is Real

Here's the part that surprises most outsiders: every culture that has ever developed sincere devotion to one of these forces has been sincerely *right* — even when their names and rituals for it look nothing alike. Two communities on opposite ends of the continent, worshipping under completely different names, can be genuinely connected to the exact same force. The huge variety of religious practice across Fallmark isn't different peoples worshipping different gods. It's different peoples describing the same ten things in the only languages available to them.

The scholarly term for the forces collectively is **the Unnamed** — not because they lack names (they have thousands), but because every single name is a local truth rather than the whole one.

## The Ten Forces

Undying (death & transition) · Deepmind (knowledge & secrets) · Green (nature & the wild) · Scale (balance & order) · Forge (creation & craft) · Loom (fate & pattern) · Wanderer (the sea & journeys) · Hollow (grief & longing) · Mask (identity & performance) · Ember (courage & sacrifice).

Most people in the world don't think about their faith at this level of theory any more than they think about the mechanics of walking. They know a name, a shrine, a ritual their family has always kept — the underlying framework is background, not something anyone needs explained to them at the dinner table.

## What This Means for Your Character

You don't need a fully worked-out theology to play a cleric, paladin, or any character with a faith. Pick a force that resonates with who your character is — or pick a specific, local name for one of these forces that fits their culture — and the rest can develop at the table. A character doesn't need to consciously worship anything to be aligned with a force, either: someone who's spent their life standing between danger and the people they love is Ember-aligned whether they've ever set foot in a shrine or not.
