This came directly out of the pre-Session-Zero survey. This is what you told me you wanted, reflected back so we're all working from the same page.

<div class="callout">
<div class="callout-title">The tone</div>
<ul>
<li><strong>No safety nets, no handrails, no chosen-one framing.</strong> You can end up in genuinely deadly situations without a built-in solution. Find one, or don't. Either way, it stands.</li>
<li><strong>Enemies play to win.</strong> Real tactics, real stakes. Don't expect pulled punches by default.</li>
<li><strong>NPCs can be difficult.</strong> Aggressive, rude, unfair. Not every NPC is likeable, and that's on purpose.</li>
<li><strong>Expect more descriptive environment and scene-setting</strong> than you might be used to. Places should feel distinct from each other.</li>
<li><strong>Combat, downtime, and character-driven moments</strong> are what this table wants most. Expect prep to weight accordingly.</li>
</ul>

None of this is about making the game miserable. It's the opposite of softening; you asked for a world with real teeth, and that's what we're building.

## A few house rules

These are mostly carried over from the last campaign. They worked, so we're keeping them. Full mechanics for anything unfamiliar will get walked through properly at Session Zero.

### Rolls and checks

- **Secret rolls.** Sometimes I'll roll Perception, Insight, or similar checks for you, secretly, behind the screen, when whether you *failed* shouldn't be visible to you as a player. If your character would have no way of knowing they missed something, you shouldn't either.
- **Inspiration.** Instead of granting advantage, inspiration lets you reroll after you've already rolled but before you know the result. Keep either roll.
- **Skill check help.** You can only help another player on a check if you're proficient in that skill too, unless you can convince me a different skill you're proficient in would genuinely apply here.
- **Automatic success.** A genuinely brilliant plan or a great piece of role-play can just work outright. I'll call it when I see it.
- **Degrees of success and failure.** How well you succeed or fail can shape the outcome, not just whether you did.
- **Max crit dice.** On a critical hit, the extra damage dice from the crit are maxed instead of rolled.
- **Rule of cool.** If you've got a genuinely fun, clever, or cinematic idea, go for it. The rules can bend for a moment like Legolas surfing a shield down a staircase while firing arrows.

### Combat, injury, and recovery

- **Lower initiative.** Any player can choose to lower their initiative in combat. Once changed, that's your initiative for the rest of that fight.
- **Flanking.** Two creatures on opposite sides of, and adjacent to, a single target count as flanking it. Either one can spend their reaction to give the other a +2 bonus on its next attack against that target.
- **Exhaustion.** Each level of exhaustion gives you a -2 penalty on saves, checks, and attack rolls.
- **0 HP.** At 0 HP you're dying: you can still crawl up to 5 feet, but you can't stand up or take normal actions. Death saves work as usual. Dropping to 0 HP also gives you 1 level of exhaustion. A long rest removes 1 level of exhaustion, a genuinely comfortable long rest (a real bed, somewhere safe) removes 2.
- **Lingering injuries.** If you drop to 0 HP, or take a single hit for more than half your max HP, you roll on the injury table, automatically, no save to avoid it. These heal with a week of low-level healing magic, or a single casting of a 6th-level or higher healing spell.
- **Potion use.** Drinking a healing potion, or feeding one to someone else, can be an action or a bonus action. An action gets the potion's full healing, a bonus action means rolling for it instead.
- **Excursion resting.** We're using a specific variant resting system rather than vanilla short and long rests. It worked well before, and we're keeping it. One addition: outside somewhere genuinely safe, making camp isn't automatic. Someone rolls Survival to actually secure the site before a rest counts in full.
- **A limiter on certain "safety net" spells.** Spells like *Goodberry* or *Tiny Hut* that would otherwise fully remove a survival pressure get a small built-in limiter, similar to how Guidance works. Exact details still to be confirmed at the table.

### Character and world

- **I know a guy.** Every now and then (when it makes sense and per the DM's discretion), you can announce that your character knows somebody who can help with whatever's happening right now. That NPC has to make sense for your backstory and the situation, but once you name them, they're real: a permanent part of the world and campaign from that point on.
- **Magic item identification.** Magic items don't identify themselves the moment you pick them up. You'll need to cast *Identify*, or spend time experimenting with the item, to learn what it actually does.
- **Weapon mastery.** We're using the 2024 ("5.5e") weapon mastery properties.

A couple of specific character-build choices from the last campaign are intentionally being left out this time. The exact list will be confirmed at Session Zero rather than guessed at here.

## Coming into Session Zero

Come with a character concept, not necessarily a finished character. Mechanical details are easy to lock down once we're all in the room together. What's more useful to bring: a sense of who your character is, why they'd end up adventuring, and (if you already have thoughts) how they might connect to the other characters at the table.
