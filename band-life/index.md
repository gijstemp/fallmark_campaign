In Aethermere, adventuring isn't a strange or marginal career. It's a recognized trade with its own culture, gossip, and pecking order, closer to how a legendary knight's household or a master guild company gets talked about than anything modern. Bands earn their name the old way: through witnessed deeds, word of mouth, and the songs and penny dreadfuls that outlast the deed itself.

## How Bands Form

There are three recognized "usual" paths into the life, but other options are possible:

- **Organic.** A few people survive something together, and keep going. They might lose someone and then gain another. The most celebrated origin story in every legend, for good reason: it's inefficient and occasionally fatal, but it produces the realest chemistry when it works.
- **Mentor system.** A famous retired adventurer takes on a protégé. Being chosen this way is a real, early mark of credibility, though the mentor's name follows the new band around for a while, which cuts both ways.
- **Guild halls.** Formal institutions found in most sizeable cities, offering training and contract access. Quality varies enormously: a capital's guild hall has real Consortium connections; a small-town one is closer to an organized noticeboard.

However your band actually came together is entirely your call at the table.

## The Reputation Ladder

Your band's public name (what innkeepers know, what clients expect, what the rumor mill says) moves on a rough four-tier scale:

| Standing | What It Means |
|----------|----------------|
| **Local** | Known in a town or a valley. Credentials, but no name yet. |
| **Regional** | Your name has started to travel without you. |
| **Continental** | A household name across Fallmark. |
| **Legendary** | The tier that barely exists. |

Nobody starts anywhere near the top. Nobody skips the small, unglamorous jobs either. Every legendary band did a hundred things that never made it into any song before they were legendary.

## Where You're Starting

Your band is forming in **Coppervane**, a small foothill town, not a big city. The jobs will be modest, personal, and local at first: the kind of work that reveals who your characters actually are before the stakes get complicated. The wider world (bigger cities, bigger names, bigger trouble) is coming. Just not on day one.
